Our Process

Evaluation Methodology

When a clinician is mid-encounter with a patient, their attention belongs in the room — not on a confusing interface. Human factors evaluation is the science of designing tools that work with human cognition rather than against it. We offer a structured, multi-phase heuristic evaluation framework designed to systematically assess usability within a clinically grounded workflow, using established human factors principles and independent expert review.

How we evaluate

Our evaluation follows a four-phase process — moving from workflow definition to expert heuristic analysis, structured synthesis, and clinical validation. Each phase builds on the last.

01

Clinical Workflow Development & Validation

An 8-step end-to-end workflow was defined through interviews and direct observation of primary care providers in outpatient clinical settings. Validated by a physician with dual expertise in clinical practice and clinical informatics to ensure applicability as a standardized reference.

02

Independent Heuristic Evaluation

Two human factors experts conducted independent, blinded evaluations of each product against Nielsen's 10 heuristics at every workflow step. Issues were documented with associated heuristic violations and assigned severity ratings on Nielsen's 4-point scale.

03

Structured Synthesis & Visualization

Evaluators convened to reconcile discrepancies and produce a consolidated list of usability issues with final severity ratings. Findings were organized by workflow stage, heuristic category, and severity to identify patterns and concentrations of higher-severity findings.

04

Clinical SME Validation

The physician-clinical informatics expert who validated the workflow in Phase 1 conducted a structured, task-based review of each product, following the same 8-step workflow to corroborate or challenge heuristic findings within authentic clinical context.

We start by understanding the workflow — exactly as it happens in the exam room.

The 8-step clinical workflow

This workflow was developed through clinician interviews and direct observation in outpatient primary care, reflecting actual clinical practice rather than idealized system use. It served as the standardized reference frame for every evaluation.

Step 1

Pre-charting & Preparation

Clinician reviews prior notes and sets up the session before the patient arrives.

Step 2

Patient Consent

AI recording is disclosed and patient consent is obtained before audio capture begins.

Step 3

Initiating Recording

Clinician starts ambient audio capture through the scribe interface.

Step 4

Patient Encounter

The clinical visit proceeds; the AI scribe captures conversation in the background.

Step 5

Pausing & Resuming

Clinician pauses recording during non-clinical portions and resumes as needed.

Step 6

Reviewing & Editing

Clinician reviews the AI-generated note and makes edits for accuracy and completeness.

Step 7

EHR Upload

The finalized note is transferred into the electronic health record.

Step 8

Final Review & Sign-off

Clinician performs a final review in the EHR and signs the completed note.

With the workflow established, we apply evidence-based heuristic evaluations at every step.

Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics — applied in clinical context

Each product is evaluated against Nielsen's 10 heuristics at every step of the workflow. These principles are the gold standard for identifying usability issues, adapted here with a clinical lens.

H1

Visibility of System Status

The system communicates its current state — recording, processing, and uploading — so clinicians always know what is happening.

H2

Match Between System and Real World

Language, concepts, and workflows align with clinical practice and conventions familiar to the intended users.

H3

User Control and Freedom

Clinicians can undo, pause, and exit actions easily without being trapped in unintended states during documentation.

H4

Consistency and Standards

Interface elements, terminology, and controls behave predictably and consistently across all workflow stages.

H5

Error Prevention

The system is designed to prevent errors before they occur, particularly during recording, editing, and EHR submission.

H6

Recognition Rather Than Recall

Clinicians rely on visible cues rather than memory — especially important in time-pressured clinical settings.

H7

Flexibility and Efficiency of Use

Experienced users can navigate the system quickly without compromising safety or completeness of documentation.

H8

Aesthetic and Minimalist Design

Interfaces present only relevant information; unnecessary elements do not compete with critical clinical content.

H9

Help Users Recognize, Diagnose, and Recover from Errors

Error messages use plain clinical language and guide users to corrective action.

H10

Help and Documentation

Support materials are available, searchable, and relevant to the clinical context in which they are needed.

Every issue is rated on a 4-point severity scale

Each usability issue identified during evaluation is assigned a severity rating. This guides the prioritization of findings and makes it easy to understand the clinical impact of each issue at a glance.

0

Not a Problem

No usability concern identified at this workflow point.

1

Cosmetic

Fix only if time and resources permit. Low clinical impact.

2

Minor

Low priority to fix. Causes friction but does not impede task completion.

3

Major

High priority. Significant friction; important to fix before deployment.

4

Critical

Imperative to fix. Substantially impedes safe or effective use. Patient safety risk.

Expert analysis alone isn't enough. We validate findings against work as it's actually done — not as it's imagined.

Clinical Validation

We ask a practicing clinician to do the work

Heuristic evaluation is a powerful method, but it is most valuable when paired with authentic clinical use. After our experts complete their independent evaluations, a physician conducts a structured, task-based review of each product.

Following the same 8-step workflow used throughout the evaluation, this subject matter expert reviews every tool as a practicing clinician would. From pre-charting through reviewing the final note. Their role is to corroborate, challenge, and add context to our heuristic findings, ensuring that what we identify as a usability problem actually matters in the real clinical environment.

This supports the difference between work as imagined and work as done.

All of that work leads to a single, clear grade. Designed to make your decision easier.

Grading

Our evaluation findings are translated into a clear, structured grade — giving you a straightforward way to compare tools and understand where each one excels or falls short.
